Reference Card

The UDPCT method

Blank editor → working solution, no snippets. Separate thinking from typing: do the first four rows before you write real code.

StepWhat you doThe question to ask
U — UnderstandRestate inputs & outputs. Write 2–3 examples + an edge case (empty, single element).“What exactly goes in, what exactly comes out?”
D — Do by handSolve one example manually, narrating every move aloud. No code.“What is my hand actually doing, move by move?”
P — PatternName the repeated behaviour: what you track, and when you act. Write plain-English steps.“What did I keep doing? What did I keep tracking?”
C — CodeTranslate each plain-English step into one line. Mechanical.“Which line does this step become?”
T — TestRun your examples through it, edge case first. A failure points at the wrong step.“Does it match every example I wrote in U?”
The one rule
If you’re reaching for a snippet, you skipped D. Go back and trace an example by hand. The algorithm lives in your hand-trace, not on the internet.
Common tracked-things (from step P)
a running count · a running total / max / min · a previous value to compare against · two pointers (indices) moving through data · a seen set/map · a result accumulator. Most simple problems are one or two of these.
Two triggers that mean “flush / finalise after the loop”
If your hand acts when something changes (like RLE), the last group never triggers a change — so handle it once after the loop ends.

Source: George Pólya, How to Solve It.  ·  Taught in Lesson 1.
